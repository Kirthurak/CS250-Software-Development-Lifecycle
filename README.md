# CS 250 – Software Development Lifecycle

## About This Repository

This repository contains my portfolio work for CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle at Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU).

Throughout this course, I explored Agile methodologies, the Scrum framework, and the importance of communication and teamwork in software development.

## Portfolio Artifact – Sprint Review and Retrospective

My Sprint Review and Retrospective document examines the SNHU Travel project, where the Scrum team developed a travel booking application. It reflects on the responsibilities of the Product Owner, Scrum Master, developer, and tester, alongside the challenges of changing requirements and the importance of continuous improvement.

## Reflection

### 1. How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I begin by understanding the user's needs and identifying the problems the software should solve. User stories help translate these requirements into clear, manageable tasks by explaining what the user wants and why it matters.

During the SNHU Travel project, I learnt how user stories guide development and testing. They also help ensure that the finished application meets the user's expectations.

### 2. How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach software development by understanding the requirements, planning the design, writing the code, and testing each feature. Breaking larger tasks into smaller steps helps me remain organised and identify problems early.

In future projects, I hope to incorporate Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Reviews, and Retrospectives. These Agile practices encourage regular communication, flexibility, and continuous improvement.

### 3. What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

I believe a good team member should be reliable, respectful, cooperative, and willing to learn. Clear communication, accountability, and constructive feedback are essential to achieving shared goals.

The SNHU Travel project taught me that successful software development depends not only on technical knowledge but also on teamwork, adaptability, and mutual understanding.

## Conclusion

CS 250 has strengthened my understanding of Agile development and the software development life cycle. I intend to apply these lessons throughout my computer science studies and future career as a software engineer.
